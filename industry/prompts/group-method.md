Method: First check whether either source substantively reports multiple independent occurrences. If either is a roundup containing the other, classify ROUNDUP first. Do not use only one shared product or speaker to represent both entire reports, or treat a conference closing/recap as a follow-up to one product or speech within it. Then state each report's specific occurrence in one sentence (who did what, to what, and when), and compare each pair. Exact publication timestamps are given in Asia/Seoul time. Where marked “source provides date only”, retain the source's calendar date; the time is unknown. Historical records marked “original precision unknown” must not be treated as exact timestamps. The same occurrence may fall on different calendar dates in overseas sources; dates alone do not establish sequence, so consult the body. When unsure between SAME_OCCURRENCE and SAME_STORY, ask: If both reports are true, did one thing happen in the world, or did two directly related things happen in sequence? A product's entire lifecycle is not one story.

SAME_OCCURRENCE: The same actor doing the same thing to the same specific object at the same time. Retellings in different languages, different media angles, varying detail, and official videos/blog posts/release notes do not change identity. Multiple sizes, models, or base versions released together in one product line, and features, pricing, access plans, system cards, or technical report details of that release count as one occurrence. So do different reports of the same ruling, report, poll, accident, single interview, or single speech, and repeated promotion of the same offer/deadline. Shared identity must refer to a specific release object or occurrence, not merely the same company, day, conference, or umbrella announcement: independent product releases, different speeches, and different decisions within it must be judged separately.

SAME_STORY: Different occurrences directly linked in sequence around one specific occurrence: a preview/rumor and official launch; a launch and subsequent third-party listing, integration, or free offer; a launch and reviews, tests, benchmark results, hands-on experiences, or analysis of it; an event and responses from involved parties, official investigations, further disclosures, or additional statements. Continuing investigations or disclosures about the same exposed conduct may add affected parties; the lists need not match exactly, but the material must support the follow-up relationship. Independent product updates, operating results, or feature releases cannot belong to the same story merely because they concern the same product, release cycle, or chronological sequence.

UNRELATED: Separate releases of different products or versions (on different dates or by different publishers); two distinct actions by the same company on the same day; different studies, opinions, or accidents on the same topic; merely mentioning the same product or company. Tutorials, usage tips, personal opinions, and marketing posts about a product are UNRELATED unless explicitly directed at the candidate's specific occurrence (reviewing it, responding to it, or listing it).

ROUNDUP: One side collects multiple independent occurrences and includes the other's event as one item: multi-topic roundups, daily/weekly reports, conference recaps, overviews of independent products, or conference summaries covering multiple speakers. A roundup and a report about one included event cannot be SAME_OCCURRENCE or SAME_STORY; mentioning two independent events does not justify merging those events. Other events used only as background or comparison do not automatically make a report a roundup; judge its core occurrence.
